Read-only scientific assessment, 27 September 2026. **V2 is frozen.** This document adds no experiment, metric, model fit, embedding, configuration, or change to V2 artifacts. `FACT` denotes direct code/artifact evidence; `INFERENCE` denotes interpretation; `RECOMMENDATION` denotes a future protocol proposal, not authorization to execute it. ## 1. Executive decision summary

**FACT.** There are four distinct implemented objectives, two in-batch distribution-matching objectives and two sampled-triplet objectives. The behavior sampler draws one different-window same-condition positive per anchor and an independent negative from the unrestricted valid split. The time sampler uses the exact same-trial `time_id + 10` positive and unrestricted same-split negatives. The sampled-triplet loss has an alignment term plus a **negative-only** log-sum-exp term; calling it conventional positive-in-denominator InfoNCE would misstate the code. See Sections 2–3.

**FACT.** The existing diagnostic CSV covers 3 seeds × 2 architectures × 4 objectives × 5 domain/population units × validation/test = 240 rows. The 120 validation rows support a pre-tuning geometric rule. Transformer near-collapse is configuration- and seed-dependent, not universal. Same-condition negatives are a property of the current objective, not automatically a software bug. Current real runner files do not match the historical source hashes saved with a representative V2 run.

**RECOMMENDATION.** A conditional **GO to design** a validation-only HPO protocol is defensible after explicitly freezing the objective interpretation, anti-collapse rule, provenance snapshot, and selection firewall below. This is **not GO to train**, not approval to change an objective, and not a claim of an uncontaminated future test on the already-inspected V2 split. No numeric HPO search space is selected here.

Raw validation-loss levels across the four objectives are not on a common scale.

## 3. Sampling semantics

**FACT — behavior.** `CEBRASupervisedWindowDataset` restricts the candidate pool to supplied valid indices in one split. For each eligible anchor it uniformly samples a *different* positive within its label group, and an independent negative uniformly from the **whole** candidate pool, with replacement (`src/neurobridge/sampling/cebra_behavior.py:47–100`). Thus `p(n has anchor condition | anchor class c)=n_c/N`. Averaged over anchors from that pool, the expected rate is `Σ_c(n_c/N)²`. The Phase-1 audit computed 0.1251 for Synthetic A/B train and 0.1303 for real total-65 train from saved Stage-2 labels; the real train class counts are `[13340,11020,9280,11020,7540,10440,8120,6960]`, `N=77720`. For 1024 negative columns this is approximately 128 or 133 same-condition columns *per anchor in expectation*, with repeats possible. A row's own negative can be the anchor or its positive (each probability `1/N` for that draw); a different row's negative can also coincide. Exact realized counts vary.

**FACT — time.** `CEBRATripletWindowDataset` looks up `(trial_id, time_id+10)` among valid, same-split candidates; there is one designated positive per eligible anchor (`src/neurobridge/sampling/cebra_time.py:42–75`). Negatives are drawn with replacement from the unrestricted valid same-split pool (`:85–98`): the same trial, nearby time, or same condition is allowed. The code tries to avoid a row's **own** anchor/positive by replacing an initially colliding pool index with the next pool index when pool size exceeds two. That one-step replacement is not a general exclusion proof: it is not rechecked against the *other* forbidden index, and, crucially, another row's negative column can equal this anchor/positive. Therefore the effective B-column denominator can contain identical or temporally adjacent data identities. The `+10` training relation is not the imposed cross-population A/B lag. A 21-bin, stride-1 window shifted 10 centers shares 11 raw bins with its positive, but this overlap stays inside a trial/split (Phase-1 audit Sections 6 and 11).

**INFERENCE.** Unrestricted marginal negatives define a conditional-versus-marginal discrimination problem. They need not enforce all same-condition points as an equivalence class; in time, the learner is asked to prefer an exact `+10` relation despite nearby or same-trial negatives. Neither fact alone establishes the cause of any failed configuration.

## 5. Behavior contrastive decision

**FACT.** Positive distribution = other valid windows conditional on the anchor's discrete condition; negative distribution = valid-split marginal, not “different condition only” (Section 3). Same-condition negatives are therefore expected at the observed class-frequency rate. The two training signals are not logically inconsistent under conditional-versus-marginal noise discrimination: a same-condition positive is *designated* as positive, while a marginal sample may by chance share its label.

**INFERENCE.** If the intended scientific construct is strict condition-invariant geometry, same-condition negatives can exert opposing gradients and make that construct harder to learn. The CSV shows some near-collapse, but **CAUSE UNKNOWN**; neither this sampler nor temperature has been isolated causally. Conversely, the multi-positive supervised loss expresses the equivalence-class interpretation directly and provides an existing, non-identical comparison. It differs in both formula and candidate distribution, so it is not a one-factor sampler ablation.

**RECOMMENDATION / scientific choice.** Keep the V2 behavior objective under its exact current definition for historical interpretation. An eventual `behavior-v2` with same-condition positives and **different-condition-only** negatives would be a **NEW SCIENTIFIC OBJECTIVE**, requiring a separate named protocol, run IDs, and prospective comparison/approval. It is not a silent bug fix and is not implemented here.

## 6. Time contrastive decision

**FACT.** The current `time-v1` target is one same-trial partner at `t+10`, not symmetric ±10, a neighborhood, a soft temporal kernel, or the imposed inter-population delay. Its unrestricted same-split marginal can draw same-trial, nearby, same-condition, or cross-trial negatives; the row-level avoidance has the limited semantics in Section 3. Boundary/validity checks determine which anchors have a partner.

**INFERENCE.** This samples a specific directed temporal relation; a model may learn it without preserving an externally imposed A/B lag or full temporal geometry. Overlapping windows may make the chosen relation comparatively easy. Existing lag failures do not prove a sampling/index bug; Phase-1 found no direct cross-trial or padding route in the inspected code. **CAUSE UNKNOWN** for near-collapse and lag-pathology mechanisms.

**RECOMMENDATION / scientific choice.** A later named `time-v2` neighborhood, symmetric, or soft relation would be a scientifically meaningful ablation only after its hypothesis, candidate distribution, and readouts are specified. It changes supervision and cannot be retroactively substituted for V2 or silently folded into hyperparameter tuning. No implementation here.

## 7. Collapse/anisotropy diagnostic rule

**RECOMMENDATION — freeze before any Phase-2 HPO.** Apply the following **validation-only** geometric checks to unit-sphere `N×3` embeddings on the same prespecified valid validation-window scope for every candidate; fixed pair-sampling RNG 100 and at most 512 rows (existing CSV convention). This is a descriptive eligibility diagnostic, not a representation-quality score. Calculate finite coordinate SDs, covariance eigenvalues `λ1≥λ2≥λ3≥0`, trace `T=Σλ`, singular scales `sqrt(λj)`, PR `T²/Σλ²`, effective rank `exp(-Σ(λj/T)ln(λj/T))`, PC1 `λ1/T`, PC1+2 `(λ1+λ2)/T`, cosine and Euclidean q05/q50/q95, and fraction `f01` of sampled pair distances `<0.01`. A zero trace makes PR/ER undefined, not “good”. Test rows are **not** used to calibrate or apply a selection constraint.

1. **Invalid artifact:** wrong shape, row/metadata mismatch, nonfinite embedding/diagnostic, or failed unit norm tolerance. Treat as qualification failure, not a scientific near-collapse label.
2. **Probable near-collapse:** simultaneously `distance_q50 < 0.02`, `f01 ≥ 0.25`, and covariance trace `< 0.001`. A median distance <0.02 on the unit sphere means many rows are almost coincident; the near-pair fraction and absolute variance confirm that PR alone is not driving the call. Borderline cases crossing a threshold remain reported as borderline, not discarded silently.
3. **Broadly dispersed:** `distance_q50 ≥ 0.1`, `f01 < 0.05`, trace `≥ 0.01`, and PR `≥ 2.5` in 3D. This says spread across several axes, **not** good task/latent/temporal fidelity.
4. **Otherwise:** noncollapsed but anisotropic/effectively lower-dimensional or intermediate. Report eigen spectrum, PR/ER, PC fractions, and pair quantiles rather than treating low PR as collapse. `soft` commonly sits here: high cosine similarity and PR near 2, yet median distance ~0.18–0.26, much larger than the near-collapse regime.

**FACT / caveat.** The threshold values are a *prospective rule proposed now*, informed by already seen exploratory V2 **validation** distributions; they are not an independent blind diagnostic validation. They must not be adjusted after viewing Phase-2 candidates or test results. Existing V2 test outcomes have also been seen by investigators, so a later claim of an untouched confirmatory test requires genuinely new independent evaluation. Coordinate axes may rotate across seeds; their SDs should be preserved per seed, not averaged into a purported invariant. For synthetic A/B the audit CSV uses saved `lag_valid` for both populations; for real R0 it uses Stage-5-like validation/test center-window support, including trial-edge windows. Preserve these cohort definitions and state them in comparisons (Phase-1 audit Section 10).

## 10. Validation/test firewall

**RECOMMENDATION.** Phase-2 design must specify `TRAIN → VALIDATION → HYPERPARAMETER SELECTION`, with held-out `TEST → final evaluation only`. Keep the trial-level split and current branch semantics explicit: held-out validation can select checkpoints/hyperparameters; full-sample refit is descriptive and has no independent validation/test generalization claim. Within an architecture/objective, the default candidate selection is **lowest eligible validation objective loss**, subject to the frozen Section-7 qualification/near-collapse exclusion. Prespecify tie-breaking (for example fewer updates/parameters only after materially equal validation loss), budget, seed aggregation, failure treatment, and whether failure rate is a separate reported outcome before launching HPO. The geometry constraint guards against a low-loss but near-constant representation; it does **not** optimize geometry or combine the three thesis axes into a score. A validation loss by itself cannot certify preservation of Z, task accessibility, or temporal fidelity, and numerical losses across different objectives cannot be compared as a selection tournament. Architectures and objectives remain explicit experimental factors unless a separately approved scientific selection question is defined.

**Prohibited selection information:** held-out test decoding/geometry/lag, any held-out test-based choice of learning rate, temperature, model capacity, loss, sampler, checkpoint or stopping change; in Synthetic, ground-truth Z, Procrustes/RSA/CKA-to-Z, and held-out lag must remain scientific outcomes, not HPO targets. Validation-only anti-collapse uses validation embeddings from a train-fitted checkpoint, not the test subset. Inspecting the frozen V2 test in Phase 1 does not create a software leak, but it **does** mean an identical future held-out split is no longer an untouched confirmatory set if human choices respond to what was seen. A new independently held-out confirmation would be needed for a strong post-tuning generalization claim; do not quietly relabel existing V2 test as fresh.
